# Mistral, DeepSeek, Falcon, Qwen — Detection-Oriented Research

## Overview

Covers six models from four vendors: Mistral AI (mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2, mixtral-8x7b-instruct-v0.1, ministral-8b-instruct-2410), DeepSeek (deepseek-r1-distill-qwen-32b), TII UAE (falcon3-10b-instruct), and Alibaba (qwen1.5-72b-chat-8bit). These form the most architecturally diverse group in the PAN25 dataset.

---

## Shared RLHF Vocabulary (All Six Models)

All six models exhibit elevated frequency of the following words/phrases due to RLHF annotators systematically preferring them (documented in arXiv:2412.11385, arXiv:2508.01930):

**Words:** `crucial`, `important`, `ensure`, `leverage`, `comprehensive`, `nuanced`, `delve`, `furthermore`, `moreover`

**Phrases:**
- "plays a vital role", "stands as a testament to", "underscores the importance of"
- "It is important to note that", "It's worth mentioning that", "It is crucial to"
- "Furthermore,", "Moreover,", "Additionally,", "In addition,"
- "In conclusion,", "To summarize,", "In summary,"
- Closing offers: "Feel free to ask if you need further clarification", "Let me know if you need more details"

**Structural universals:**
- Rule of three: "A, B, and C" constructions; three bullet points; three supporting examples
- Over-completeness: answers implied sub-questions the user did not ask
- Markdown over-application: formatting in contexts where plain text would be more natural

## 1. `mistral-7b-instruct-v0.2`

**HuggingFace:** https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2
**Paper:** arXiv:2310.06825

### Architecture & Training

- 7B param dense transformer, BF16. Context: 32k tokens (4x larger than v0.1; RoPE-theta scaled to 1e6).
- v0.2 removes sliding-window attention entirely — full attention across 32k window.
- Fine-tuned from Mistral-7B-v0.2 base via SFT + DPO (no PPO/classical RLHF).
- **No moderation mechanisms** — outputs are unfiltered unlike GPT-family.
- **Tokenizer:** `mistral_common` v1, SentencePiece-based, ~32k vocabulary. Note: HuggingFace `transformers` tokenizer does NOT produce bit-identical results to official `mistral_common` — known discrepancy.
- Chat template: `[INST] ... [/INST]` bracket format. No native system prompt support.

### Stylistic Tendencies

- **Direct and terse:** responses shorter and more direct than comparable GPT-4 or Llama-2-Chat outputs.
- **Lower sycophancy:** DPO training produces less sycophantic behavior than Llama 3 under same alignment pressure — less "Great question!", "Certainly!".
- **Moderate markdown:** uses bullet lists and headers, but less aggressively than ChatGPT, especially when not explicitly instructed.
- **Fewer unsolicited safety disclaimers** than OpenAI-aligned models.
- **Instruction token artifacts:** `[INST]`/`[/INST]` tokens occasionally echo in prompt injections or unusual inputs — detectable.
- "As an AI language model..." almost entirely absent from outputs.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **Perplexity:** low when scored against Mistral-7B-base (5–15 range vs human text >30).
- **Burstiness:** flat profile — uniform per-sentence information density vs human variance.
- **Vocabulary:** shared RLHF overuse vocabulary (see above) + transition overuse: "Furthermore," "Moreover,".
- **Sentence length:** relatively short median (15–20 words). Shorter, crisper responses than same-generation competitors.
- **Format sensitivity:** outputs degrade noticeably if `[INST]`/`[/INST]` format is omitted — produces fragmented stream-of-consciousness text easier to detect.
- **No system prompt support:** causes formatting artifacts when deployed via OpenAI-compatible APIs that inject a system prompt.

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- arXiv:2503.04765 (academic writing comparison): Mistral detectable as AI-generated by standard detectors with high confidence.
- arXiv:2503.01659 (stylistic fingerprints): Mistral outputs showed mixed attribution — some classified as "Llama-like," reflecting shared open training lineage.
- **Generally easier to detect** than GPT-4 at short lengths — more predictable token sequences, smaller vocabulary applied more uniformly.
- **Evasion:** low temperature (T=0.0) produces trivially detectable output. T=0.7+ more naturalistic but still flagged by stylometric classifiers.

## 2. `mixtral-8x7b-instruct-v0.1`

**HuggingFace:** https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1
**Paper:** arXiv:2401.04088

### Architecture & Training

- **Sparse MoE:** 8 expert FFN blocks per layer, router activates **2 of 8 per token**.
- Total params: ~47B; active params per token: ~13B (inference-comparable to 13B dense; quality closer to 70B).
- Same tokenizer as Mistral-7B: `mistral_common` v1, SentencePiece, ~32k vocabulary. Same `[INST]`/`[/INST]` chat format.
- Post-training: SFT + DPO (same as Mistral-7B). Less biased than contemporaries on BBQ/BOLD benchmarks.
- At release (Jan 2024): outperformed GPT-3.5 Turbo, Claude 2.1, Gemini Pro, and Llama-2-70B-Chat on human preference benchmarks.

### Stylistic Tendencies

- **More conversational and personality-driven** than Mistral-7B. Example from model card: *"Well, I'm quite partial to a good squeeze of fresh lemon juice..."* — shows first-person voice and subjective opinion.
- **Prefers explanatory prose** over bullet-point lists for open-ended questions.
- **More verbose than Mistral-7B:** longer answers with more context and examples.
- **Better multi-turn context retention:** very consistent register and vocabulary across turns — detectable as uniform cross-turn style.
- **MoE routing artifact:** different experts specialize in different token-type subspaces → slightly higher lexical diversity at sub-sentence level vs dense models.
- **Adds prefix spaces during tokenization** — SentencePiece artifact that can cause issues when raw token IDs are inspected.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **Perplexity:** lower on technical/professional text than Mistral-7B (larger capacity); slightly higher on creative/casual text where MoE routing may produce expert conflicts.
- **Burstiness:** MoE architecture may produce slightly higher burstiness than comparable dense model — token-probability jumps at domain transitions within a response. Still below human norms.
- **Stylistic fingerprint study (arXiv:2503.01659) — KEY FINDING:** 65% of Mixtral texts produced **no agreement** among ensemble classifiers trained on Claude/Gemini/Llama/OpenAI. Only 26% attributed to OpenAI, 8.8% to Llama. **Mixtral has distinctly different stylistic properties** from all trained families — standard detectors are unreliable without Mixtral-specific training data.
- **Longer average sentences** than Mistral-7B, more embedded clauses. More frequent em-dashes and parenthetical asides.

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- **65% "no agreement" rate** is the critical finding — Mixtral-specific training data needed for robust detection.
- Standard perplexity-threshold detectors (GPT-2 or GPT-3 scoring model) may underperform — different token distribution.
- arXiv:2503.04765: Mixtral outputs still detectable as AI-generated by commercial detectors in academic writing contexts, but with lower confidence than GPT-4.
- **Evasion:** MoE routing produces more output diversity at same temperature than a dense model → slightly better evasion of watermarking and perplexity-threshold methods.

## 3. `ministral-8b-instruct-2410`

**HuggingFace:** https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Ministral-8B-Instruct-2410

### Architecture & Training

- 8B params (8,019,808,256), 36 layers. Released October 2024.
- **GQA:** 32 query heads, 8 KV heads, head dimension 128.
- **Context: 128k tokens** — dramatically larger than Mistral-7B-v0.2's 32k.
- **Ragged/interleaved sliding-window attention:** layers alternate between full attention (128k) and SWA (32k) in pattern: 128k, 32k, 32k, 32k, ... — key architectural fingerprint.
- **Tokenizer: V3-Tekken** — **131,072-token vocabulary** (4x larger than Mistral-7B's ~32k). BPE with multilingual optimization. Requires `mistral-common >= 1.4.4`.
- Same `[INST]`/`[/INST]` format but tokens numerically incompatible with Mistral-7B's tokenizer.
- Supports function calling. License: Mistral Research License (not Apache 2.0 — commercial use requires separate agreement).
- **Known infrastructure issue:** vLLM caps context at 32k; TGI has tokenizer deserialization failures. Requires `mistral-inference` library for full 128k context.

### Stylistic Tendencies

- **More structured and formal** than Mistral-7B-v0.2. 128k context → more coherent, less repetitive long-form outputs.
- **Multilingual fluency:** stronger French, German, Spanish than Mistral-7B.
- **Math/code quality** higher (HumanEval 76.8%, Math 54.5%). Mathematical explanations are step-by-step but concise — not DeepSeek-R1's verbose chain-of-thought.
- **V3-Tekken tokenizer:** fewer tokenization artifacts in technical text (chemical names, code identifiers, math notation tokenize as single tokens).
- **Less stylistic drift** in long outputs than smaller-context models — interleaved attention maintains coherence across very long spans.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **Tokenizer fingerprint:** V3-Tekken 131k vocabulary produces statistically different unigram distributions than Mistral-7B's 32k or GPT family. Character-level n-gram analysis detects different token-boundary patterns. Rare Unicode and multilingual subwords handled as single tokens → smoother multilingual text with fewer mid-word spaces.
- **Perplexity cross-scoring issue:** scoring Ministral outputs against GPT-2 or LLaMA-2 may inflate apparent perplexity → potentially evades perplexity-threshold detectors calibrated on older models.
- **Burstiness:** similar to other Mistral-family models — flat relative to human writing. Interleaved attention means very long outputs are more coherent (lower burstiness across long spans).
- **No system prompt default:** like other Mistral models, behavior can vary more than GPT-family models when system prompts are inconsistently applied.

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- No model-specific published detection studies as of April 2026 (released Oct 2024, limited literature).
- Research License limits commercial use → lower prevalence in the wild than Mistral-7B-v0.2. Underrepresented in commercial detector training sets.
- **Evasion via cross-model perplexity mismatch:** 131k vocabulary → detectors calibrated on older models may underestimate AI-generation probability.

## 4. `deepseek-r1-distill-qwen-32b`

**HuggingFace:** https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B
**Paper:** arXiv:2501.12948

### Architecture & Training

- **Base model: Qwen2.5-32B** (33B params, dense, not MoE). Context: 128k tokens. BF16. MIT license.
- **Distillation pipeline:** fine-tuned on ~800k chain-of-thought samples generated by full DeepSeek-R1 (671B MoE, 37B active params). Teacher model's reasoning traces — including extended `<think>` blocks — used as supervised training targets.
- Parent DeepSeek-R1 trained via: cold-start SFT → RL stage 1 (reasoning reward) → RL stage 2 (human preference via GRPO). Distilled version inherits reasoning style without the RL training itself.
- **No system prompts:** documentation explicitly warns against using system prompts — all instructions must be in the user turn. Hard behavioral artifact of distillation setup.
- **Recommended sampling:** temperature=0.6, top_p=0.95, max_tokens=32768. Outside 0.5–0.7: below 0.5 → endless repetition loops; above 0.7 → incoherent tangents.

### Stylistic Tendencies

Most stylistically distinctive model in the PAN25 dataset:

- **Visible chain-of-thought:** outputs begin with `<think>` ... `</think>` blocks containing extended internal reasoning before final answer. **Hard-coded format artifact and single most detectable feature.** In consumer interfaces often hidden; in raw API output always present.
- **Thinking token vocabulary inside `<think>` blocks:**
  - Initiators: "Okay,", "Hmm,", "Let me think about this.", "Alright,"
  - Self-correction: "Wait,", "Actually,", "Let me reconsider.", "I think I made an error."
  - Progress markers: "So far so good.", "Now let's move on to..."
  - Verification: "Let me verify this.", "Let me check my work.", "Does this make sense?"
  - **"Aha moment" pattern** (documented in paper): model spontaneously inserts *"Wait, I need to reconsider my approach here."* followed by extended re-analysis.
- **Final answers are structured:** math → `\boxed{answer}`; code → fenced blocks; essays → intro/body/conclusion.
- **Longer outputs overall:** RL training incentivized allocating more tokens to hard problems. Typical response lengths: 2,000–8,000 tokens including think block.
- **Language mixing:** may switch from English to Chinese mid-reasoning in edge cases. Known artifact from parent model's RL training on multilingual data.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **`<think>` tag:** single most reliable signal. If raw output available, presence of `<think>`/`</think>` is near-certain indicator of DeepSeek-R1 family.
- **arXiv:2503.01659 KEY FINDING:** 74.2% of DeepSeek-R1 outputs were classified as **OpenAI-generated** by ensemble trained on Claude/Gemini/Llama/OpenAI. R1 was aligned to OpenAI-preferred response styles → false positives: detectors designed to catch OpenAI output will also flag DeepSeek-R1 outputs.
- **Within-response perplexity gradient:** `<think>` block has notably higher perplexity (exploratory, less polished) than final answer (lower perplexity, assured). **Distinctive fingerprint** — unique among all models.
- **Response length distribution:** bimodal — either very short (when `<think>` bypassed on simple queries: `<think>\n\n</think>`) or very long (2k–32k tokens). Human writing has unimodal length distribution.
- **Burstiness:** high within `<think>` block (exploratory, highly variable structures); low in final answer (converged, polished). This **two-phase burstiness pattern** is unique.
- **Boxed answer artifact:** `\boxed{...}` in mathematical responses — near-unique fingerprint for DeepSeek-R1 family.
- **No system prompt tolerance:** inserting a system prompt causes quality degradation and format violations — detectable in adversarial probe setups.

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- When `<think>` blocks stripped (as many production interfaces do): detection becomes harder — final answer has stylistic similarity to OpenAI per arXiv:2503.01659.
- **Vulnerability:** at T=0.6 with think-block stripping, outputs closely resemble GPT-4 stylistically. Standard word-frequency detectors misclassify as GPT-4 output.
- Temperature sensitivity (degrades outside 0.5–0.7) → out-of-range outputs detectable by quality-based heuristics (repetition loops trivially detectable).
- arXiv:2503.04765: all DeepSeek outputs detectable as AI-generated by commercial detectors, but attribution to specific model vs GPT-4 unreliable.

## 5. `falcon3-10b-instruct`

**HuggingFace:** https://huggingface.co/tiiuae/Falcon3-10B-Instruct
**Blog:** https://huggingface.co/blog/falcon3

### Architecture & Training

- Dense transformer, 40 decoder blocks.
- **GQA:** 32 query heads, 4 KV heads, **head dimension 256** (unusually wide vs standard 64 or 128 — deliberate TII choice for long-range attention quality).
- RMSNorm + SwiGLU activation (same as Llama-3 family). Context: 32k tokens. RoPE base 1,000,042.
- **Vocabulary: 131,072 tokens.**
- **Pretraining:** 2T tokens of web, code, STEM, and multilingual data on 1,024 H100 GPUs. Architecture depth-scaled from Falcon3-7B-Base.
- **Post-training:** 1.2M samples across STEM, conversational, code, safety, function calling. No published DPO vs RLHF details.
- **Languages:** English, French, Spanish, Portuguese only (narrower than Mistral or Qwen).
- License: TII Falcon-LLM License 2.0 (not Apache 2.0). Released December 2024.

### Stylistic Tendencies

- **Default system prompt identity:** "You are a helpful friendly assistant Falcon3 from TII, try to follow instructions as much as possible." → outputs often echo identity: *"As Falcon3 from TII, I can help you with..."* — distinctive stylistic artifact.
- **Strong instruction-following orientation** (IFEval 78.17%). Numbered lists when asked, code blocks for code, concise answers when prompted for brevity.
- **STEM-heavy responses:** step-by-step breakdowns for quantitative questions with intermediate calculations.
- **More formal register** than Mistral-7B-v0.2; less personality-driven than Mixtral. Utilitarian and task-focused.
- **Less creative variance** than broadly trained models — narrower 4-language scope + STEM-heavy post-training.
- **Tool use:** function calling responses use strict JSON format (BFCL AST score: 90.5%) — trivially machine-detectable in those contexts.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **GQA head width artifact:** 256-dimension KV heads (vs 64 or 128 in comparable models) → different attention pattern detectable in embedding-space fingerprinting — not visible in surface text.
- **131k vocabulary:** same size as Ministral-8B — cleaner tokenization of technical terms. Fewer multilingual boundary artifacts than Mistral-7B.
- **Perplexity cross-scoring:** like Ministral, scoring Falcon3 outputs against GPT-2 or LLaMA-2 may inflate apparent perplexity.
- **Burstiness:** flat relative to human writing. Non-English outputs (French/Spanish/Portuguese) are fluent and low-burstiness in-domain; out-of-domain languages (German, Arabic) show quality drops and higher perplexity — detectable as model-specific failures.
- **Response structure:** strong tendency toward bullet-pointed lists and numbered steps. Headers (`##`) used more than Mistral-7B, less than GPT-4.
- **Safety layer:** will refuse clearly harmful requests with standardized brief refusals: "I'm unable to assist with that request."

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- No model-specific published detection studies as of April 2026.
- Narrow deployment footprint (TII license) → likely underrepresented in commercial detector training sets.
- **Identity artifact:** if default system prompt used, "Falcon3 from TII" may appear in outputs — trivial detection signal.
- **Distinct architecture** (GQA-256 heads, TII-specific training) → stylometric classifiers trained on other models may produce lower confidence attributions.

## 6. `qwen1.5-72b-chat-8bit`

**HuggingFace (chat):** https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen1.5-72B-Chat
**GPTQ-Int8 variant:** https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen1.5-72B-Chat-GPTQ-Int8
**Blog:** https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen1.5/

### Architecture & Training

- Dense transformer, decoder-only, 72B params.
- SwiGLU activation, RoPE, **attention QKV bias** (unusual — most recent models drop attention biases; this is a Qwen-family fingerprint).
- GQA — reduces KV cache for 72B inference.
- **Mixed sliding-window and full attention layers** (similar principle to Ministral-8B).
- Context: 32k tokens. Requires `transformers >= 4.37.0`.
- **Tokenizer: Qwen-family custom BPE, 150,000+ token vocabulary** — largest of all models studied. Optimized for Chinese, English, code, multilingual.
- **Post-training:** SFT + DPO. Alignment to human preferences across language tasks, code, math, and tool use. Released February 2024.
- **Quantization (8-bit):** GPTQ-Int8. "Near-lossless" quality retention at 72B scale — larger models more robust to quantization. Perplexity increase ~1–3% on standard benchmarks. Output distribution nearly identical to BF16.

### Stylistic Tendencies

- **Bilingual bias:** primarily English-Chinese bilingual design goal. Reliable English responses, but may occasionally insert Chinese characters in edge cases (Chinese names, terms, mixed-language prompts). Low-frequency but distinctive artifact.
- **More formal and deferential tone** than Mistral-family — closer to GPT-3.5 in alignment behavior. More polite openings and offers of additional help.
- **Strong markdown formatting:** produces well-structured markdown (headers, bold, code blocks, tables) even when not explicitly requested. Over-formats for simple questions.
- **"Helpful assistant" persona:** strong alignment → high frequency of: "I'd be happy to," "Of course,", "Certainly,".
- **Longer responses than Mistral-7B:** 72B capacity → more comprehensive answers; errs on side of completeness.
- **System prompt responsiveness:** fully supports and follows tone/persona instructions — deployment with custom persona reduces default-alignment markers.

### Detection-Relevant Features

- **Vocabulary fingerprint:** 150k+ token vocabulary → character-level n-gram profiles differ substantially from 32k-vocabulary models. Chinese characters, CJK punctuation tokenized as single tokens — no space-separated subword artifacts. Statistically distinct unigram distributions.
- **QKV bias artifact:** unusual attention bias parameters → different attention distribution detectable via embedding-space probing — not visible in surface text.
- **Perplexity cross-scoring:** scoring against GPT-2 or Mistral-7B may show elevated apparent perplexity due to vocabulary mismatch → perplexity-threshold detectors may underestimate AI-generation probability.
- **Burstiness:** very flat — one of the flattest burstiness profiles of all six models. 72B produces very smooth, confident text with low local perplexity variance. Strong positive signal for AI detection.
- **8-bit quantization effects:** negligible at 72B scale. GPTQ-Int8 round-off errors average out with larger weight matrices. Slight flattening of probability distribution in log-likelihood ratio tests — below noise floor for practical detectors.
- **Chinese training data seepage:** rare occurrence of Chinese character mid-English-sentence (e.g., *"...in traditional Chinese medicine (中医, zhōngyī)..."*) — near-unique artifact among predominantly English-target models.
- **Over-formatting:** unsolicited bold text, headers, and tables for questions that don't require them — detectable formatting fingerprint.

### Detection Results & Vulnerabilities

- arXiv:2503.04765: Qwen outputs detectable as AI-generated by commercial detectors with confidence similar to GPT-4.
- **Stylistic similarity to GPT-family:** stylometric classifiers may attribute Qwen outputs to OpenAI family.
- **Evasion:** 150k vocabulary → KGW-style watermarking methods less effective (larger token space reduces watermark detectability at standard parameters).
- **Deployment context signal:** 8-bit quantization (24GB+ GPU) narrows real-world deployment contexts compared to Mistral-7B.
